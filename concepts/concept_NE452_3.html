NE 452 — Instrumentation and Shielding

Concept 3: Scintillation Detector Operation

Answer:

Radiation deposits energy in a scintillator material (e.g., NaI(Tl), BGO, plastic), exciting electrons to higher energy states. As electrons return to ground state, they emit visible or UV photons. This light is directed onto a photomultiplier tube (PMT) or similar device, where the photoelectric effect at the photocathode produces electrons that are amplified through a series of dynodes, producing a measurable electrical pulse proportional to the original energy deposition.
