NE 452 — Instrumentation and Shielding

Concept 2: Gas-Filled Radiation Detector Operation

Answer:

Ionizing radiation passing through the fill gas creates ion pairs (electrons and positive ions). An applied electric field between two electrodes sweeps electrons toward the anode and ions toward the cathode, producing a measurable current or voltage pulse. The magnitude of the collected charge depends on the applied voltage and operating region (ionization chamber, proportional counter, or GM tube). The pulse is processed by external electronics to count events or measure energy deposition.
