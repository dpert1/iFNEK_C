NE 355 — Nuclear Reactor Engineering

Concept 3: Positive Temperature Coefficient — Inherent Instability

Answer:

A positive temperature coefficient of reactivity means that as reactor temperature rises, reactivity also increases. This creates a positive feedback loop: higher power → higher temperature → more reactivity → even higher power. In a water-moderated or water-cooled reactor, this can occur if voiding (steam formation) increases reactivity (positive void coefficient). The reactor becomes self-reinforcing and cannot self-regulate without external intervention, making it inherently unstable. The RBMK (Chernobyl-type) reactor is the canonical example of this hazard.
