NE 474 — Radiological Safety

Concept 4: Three Subclasses of Acute Radiation Syndrome (ARS)

Answer:

  • Hematopoietic (bone marrow) syndrome: Onset at ~1–6 Gy whole-body dose. Destruction of blood-forming cells causes decreased white blood cells, platelets, and red blood cells, leading to infection, hemorrhage, and anemia. Potentially survivable with medical support.
  • Gastrointestinal (GI) syndrome: Onset at ~6–10 Gy. Destruction of intestinal epithelium causes nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and loss of the gut barrier. Fluid and electrolyte loss; nearly always fatal without aggressive treatment.
  • Central Nervous System (CNS) syndrome: Onset at >20–50 Gy. Rapid incapacitation, seizures, cardiovascular collapse. Uniformly lethal within hours to days; no effective treatment.
