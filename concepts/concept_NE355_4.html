NE 355 — Nuclear Reactor Engineering

Concept 4: Nucleate Boiling and Enhanced Heat Transfer

Answer:

In nucleate boiling, small discrete bubbles form at nucleation sites on the cladding surface and detach into the coolant. As bubbles depart, they carry latent heat away and draw cooler bulk coolant to the surface — dramatically increasing the heat transfer coefficient compared to single-phase convection. The turbulent mixing generated by bubble motion further disrupts the thermal boundary layer. This allows the cladding to transfer significantly more heat per unit area without a substantial temperature rise, as long as the heat flux remains below the critical heat flux (CHF) where a rapid transition to film boiling occurs.
