NE 300 — Fundamentals of Nuclear Engineering

Concept 2: Neutron Interactions with Matter

Answer:

There are two categories of neutron interactions: scattering and absorption. There are six subcategories:
  • Elastic scattering (n,n): Neutron collides off nucleus, transferring energy and momentum. Total kinetic energy is conserved. Primary mechanism for neutron moderation.
  • Inelastic scattering (n,n'): Neutron collides off nucleus, transferring energy and momentum. Transfers energy to the nucleus, leaving it in an excited state. Total kinetic energy is not conserved. Requires sufficient neutron energy (>keV).
  • Radiative capture (n,γ): Nucleus absorbs the neutron and emits a gamma ray as it de-excites, forming a heavier nuclide.
  • Fission (n,f): Heavy (A>230) nucleus absorbs a neutron and splits into fission fragments, releasing energy and additional neutrons.
  • Transfer (n,p) / (n,α): Neutron is absorbed, and the nucleus emits a proton or alpha particle.
  • Knockout (n,2n) / (n,np): Neutron is absorbed, and the nucleus emits a neutron and other small particle. High-energy (>MeV) neutron is required.
