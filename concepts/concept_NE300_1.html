NE 300 — Fundamentals of Nuclear Engineering

Concept 1: Three Major Radioactive Decay Processes

Answer:

  • Alpha (α) decay: The nucleus emits a helium-4 nucleus (2 protons, 2 neutrons), reducing atomic number by 2 and mass number by 4. Occurs in heavy nuclei seeking stability.
  • Beta (β) decay: Inside the nucleus, a neutron converts to a proton emitting an electron (β⁻) and antineutrino, or a proton converts to a neutron emitting a positron (β⁺) and neutrino. Atomic number (Z) changes by ±1 but atomic mass number (A) remains the same. Occurs in nuclides below (β⁻) or above (β⁺) the line of stability.
  • Gamma (γ) decay: An excited nucleus releases excess energy as a high-energy photon with no change in atomic or mass number. Often follows alpha or beta decay. Isomeric transition, not transmutation.
