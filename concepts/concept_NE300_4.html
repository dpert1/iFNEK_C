NE 300 — Fundamentals of Nuclear Engineering

Concept 4: Fissile vs. Fissionable

Answer:

  • Fissile: A nuclide capable of undergoing fission with thermal (slow) neutrons. Examples: U-235, Pu-239, U-233, Pu-241. The binding energy of the absorbed neutron alone is sufficient to exceed the fission energy threshold.
  • Fissionable: A nuclide that can undergo fission, but only when struck by fast neutrons above a threshold energy. Examples: U-238, Th-232, Pu-240. All fissile materials are fissionable, but not all fissionable materials are fissile.
