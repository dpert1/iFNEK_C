NE 355 — Nuclear Reactor Engineering

Concept 1: Four Factor Formula — Heterogeneous vs. Homogeneous

Answer:

\(k_\infty = \varepsilon p f \eta \). In a heterogeneous thermal reactor, fuel and moderator are physically separated (e.g., fuel rods in water). Compared to a homogeneous mixture:

  • ε (fast fission factor): Slightly Larger in heterogeneous — fast neutrons born in fuel see more fissionable material before escaping the fuel rod and slowing down.
  • p (resonance escape probability): Substantially Larger in heterogeneous — neutrons slow down in the moderator away from U-238 resonance absorbers, reducing resonance capture.
  • f (thermal utilization factor): Slightly Smaller in heterogeneous — moderator absorbs some thermal neutrons outside the fuel, reducing utilization.
  • η (thermal fission factor): Equal — depends on fuel composition, not geometry.
  • k\(_{\infty}\): Larger in heterogeneous overall — the gains in ε and p outweigh the loss in f.
